BU MSBA Capstone · 2025

Starbucks — Loyalty Segmentation & Choice Overload Experiment

3.2M loyalty records. A DiD causal experiment on menu complexity. A 14% retention lift that was completely invisible in aggregate — because the effect lived entirely in the middle cohort.

The Problem

Mid-frequency customers were silently churning

Top-20% customers masked the bleed in aggregate reporting. The middle cohort — highest long-term growth potential, highest sensitivity to friction — was dropping off. A bloated menu was triggering cognitive overload at the point of ordering, accelerating abandonment. Both problems were invisible in the numbers the team watched daily.

Data & Method

Clustering + DiD Causal Experiment

Segmented 3.2M loyalty records using K-means, DBSCAN, and hierarchical clustering with PCA dimensionality reduction. Then ran a Difference-in-Differences experiment — informed by Prof. Nina Mazar's behavioral economics framework on choice architecture — testing the retention effect of reducing menu complexity on the mid-frequency cohort specifically.

What I Found

14% retention uplift — invisible in aggregate

Reducing menu complexity drove a 14% projected retention lift in the mid-frequency cohort. In the aggregate view, the effect disappeared completely — masked by the stable high-frequency tier. This is exactly why aggregate dashboards are the wrong tool for cohort-level behavioral decisions.

BU MSBA Project · 2025

Airbnb — Dynamic Price Prediction Engine

Built a listing price prediction model that outperformed simple regression by capturing non-linear feature interactions — amenity bundles, neighborhood effects, and booking-window dynamics that average pricing models flatten out.

The Problem

Hosts price by gut — they leave money on the table

Most Airbnb hosts price based on what nearby listings charge and seasonal intuition. That ignores non-linear interactions: a pool adds more value in summer and in family-sized listings, but nearly zero in city studios in March. Linear regression misses all of it.

Data & Method

Gradient Boosted Regression + SHAP

Used gradient boosted regression (GBM/XGBoost) to model price as a function of listing features — room type, amenities, neighborhood, host experience, review scores, booking window. The ensemble approach captured feature interactions that linear models smooth away. SHAP values for interpretability and feature importance ranking.

What I Found

Amenity bundles drive outsized pricing power

Certain amenity combinations — washer + dryer + workspace + self check-in — predict pricing premiums far beyond the additive value of each amenity alone. Neighborhood cluster and listing type were the dominant predictors, followed by amenity bundle completeness. RMSE dropped significantly vs. the baseline linear model.

BU MSBA Project · 2025

Tesla / EV Market — Adoption Clustering & Behavioral Segmentation

Segmented the EV adopter landscape to identify which consumer profiles drive early adoption, which stall at consideration, and what behavioral and geographic features actually predict the switch from ICE to electric.

The Problem

EV adoption isn't one market — it's five

Marketing EV to "the average consumer" is a category error. Early adopters, range-anxious late majority, fleet purchasers, sustainability-driven buyers, and incentive-chasing rationalists all have distinct triggers and barriers. Treating them as one segment produces messaging that converts nobody and wastes budget on the wrong cohort.

Data & Method

K-Means + Behavioral Feature Engineering

Applied K-means clustering on consumer survey and registration data, engineered behavioral features (commute distance, home charging access, prior hybrid ownership, incentive awareness, environmental attitudes), and used PCA to reduce dimensionality before clustering. Validated cluster stability with silhouette scoring.

What I Found

Infrastructure access beats price sensitivity

Across clusters, access to home and workplace charging infrastructure dominated adoption readiness — ahead of purchase price sensitivity and environmental concern. Range anxiety was overrepresented in survey responses but underpredicted actual non-adoption in the behavioral model. Infrastructure investment unlocks adoption faster than price subsidies.

BU MSBA Project · 2025

Salesforce — CRM Pipeline Analytics & Revenue Attribution

Analyzed CRM pipeline data to identify deal velocity patterns, stage conversion bottlenecks, and the gap between forecasted and actual revenue — finding where the pipeline leaked and why.

The Problem

Forecasts looked right. Revenue didn't match.

Sales forecasts built on CRM stage probabilities assume consistent deal progression. In practice, deals stall, skip stages, and close at unexpected velocities. Without stage-level cohort analysis, the forecast model is measuring intent — not actual trajectory — and the gap between forecast and close compounds each quarter.

Data & Method

Stage Conversion Funnel + Cohort Velocity

Modeled deal flow through CRM pipeline stages using conversion rate analysis at each handoff. Built cohort-level deal velocity models — grouping deals by entry month, deal size, and rep segment — to isolate where deals were stalling vs. moving. Attribution analysis tied closed-won revenue back to top-of-funnel source at the cohort level.

What I Found

The leak was at one stage — and rep-specific

Most pipeline drop-off concentrated at a single stage transition, clustering among a subset of reps — not distributed evenly across the team. That meant the fix was coaching and process, not product or market fit. Revenue attribution also revealed one source channel was overweighted in CRM credit vs. actual contribution to close.

Google Trends — Big Data Pipeline & Demand Forecasting

Built a large-scale distributed data pipeline on Google Trends data to identify leading indicators of consumer demand shifts — signals that appear in search volume before they show up in sales data.

The Problem

Demand signals exist before purchase — nobody captures them at scale

Search volume on Google precedes purchase by days to weeks. The signal is public, real-time, and largely unused in demand forecasting because processing it at scale requires distributed big data infrastructure most analytics teams don't have or build for.

Data & Method

Apache Spark + BigQuery at scale

Ingested and processed multi-year Google Trends data across product and category keywords using Apache Spark for distributed processing and BigQuery for analytical querying. Built time-series trend decomposition to separate seasonality from genuine demand shifts, and correlated search-volume movements with downstream sales signals across categories.

What I Found

2–4 week leading indicator windows

Search volume spikes for specific long-tail queries predicted category demand shifts 2–4 weeks ahead with significant correlation — most reliably in consumer electronics and seasonal apparel. The distributed pipeline processed the full multi-year dataset in a fraction of the time of a local Python approach, validating the architecture decision.

MBTA — Transit Ridership Analytics & Operations Intelligence

Built SQL-powered analytics on MBTA ridership and operational data to surface system-level delay patterns, underserved route segments, and the relationship between schedule adherence and ridership elasticity.

The Problem

Aggregate on-time metrics masked route-level failures

System-wide on-time performance numbers were reported as acceptable. At the route level, specific segments were chronically delayed — with delay compounding across transfer points. Riders on those routes experienced a substantially different system than the headline metric suggested.

Data & Method

SQL joins across 4 tables + BI dashboards

Queried MBTA operational data using complex SQL — joining schedule, actual arrival, route, and ridership tables — to calculate segment-level delay rates, transfer compound effects, and ridership correlation with schedule variance. Built Tableau and Power BI dashboards enabling route-level drill-down on the key bottlenecks.

What I Found

Three routes drove the majority of chronic delay

A small number of route segments accounted for a disproportionate share of total system delay. Delay on these segments propagated at transfer hubs — multiplying the passenger impact. The SQL analysis also showed measurable ridership elasticity: ridership declined in direct proportion to delay frequency on chronic routes.

BU MSBA Project · 2025

Banco de Portugal — Banking Customer Segmentation

Applied unsupervised learning to banking customer data — K-means, DBSCAN, PCA, and association rule mining — to identify behavioral segments product and pricing teams could actually act on.

The Problem

Product decisions built on demographics, not behavior

Traditional bank segmentation — age, income, product holding — misses the behavioral patterns that actually predict product uptake, churn risk, and cross-sell timing. Behavioral clustering surfaces the signal that demographic bucketing hides. You end up with a segmentation you can act on.

Data & Method

K-Means + DBSCAN + PCA + ARM

Reduced feature dimensionality with PCA, then applied both K-means (partition-based) and DBSCAN (density-based) to compare cluster structures. Association Rule Mining (ARM) identified product co-holding patterns — which product combinations predict future uptake — enabling sequence-aware cross-sell recommendations with confidence thresholds.

What I Found

DBSCAN isolated churn signals K-means missed

K-means produced clean, interpretable behavioral segments. DBSCAN flagged a noise cluster that didn't fit any archetype — these customers turned out to be early-stage churn signals, not random variance. ARM revealed a high-confidence product sequence predicting term deposit uptake, enabling timed cross-sell outreach.
